# Professions & Builds

Practical archetype guides. Skill indices and training mechanics are in
`skills-reference.md`; combat mechanics in `combat.md`; step-by-step training
routes in `training-guide.md`. Pick the section matching your character's
existing skills (see `getting-started.md` §2) or the archetype you want to
grow into.

## Warrior

**Core skills:** Swordsmanship (40) *or* Macing (41) *or* Fencing (42) — pick
one weapon family, don't split. Plus Tactics (27), Anatomy (1), Healing (17),
Parrying (5).

**Why these pair:** Tactics and Anatomy both scale melee damage directly.
Healing (with bandages) is self-sufficiency — a warrior who can't patch
themselves up mid-fight is one bad pull from death. Parrying adds a passive
block chance with a shield or the right weapon type.

**Playstyle:** Melee range, `./ai/uo warmode on`, `./ai/uo attack <serial>`,
bandage between fights. See `combat.md` for the full loop and flee thresholds.

**Start:** Equip a weapon matching your chosen skill, train that weapon skill
and Tactics together (they gain fastest against live targets), buy Healing
and Anatomy to 40 from a healer NPC if gold allows.

## Mage

**Core skills:** Magery (25), Evaluate Intelligence (16), Meditation (46),
Magic Resistance (26), Inscription (23, optional but efficient for making
your own scrolls).

**Why these pair:** EvalInt scales spell damage; Meditation keeps mana flowing
between casts without needing to rest empty-handed; Magic Resistance protects
against enemy casters and gains automatically from being targeted.

**Playstyle:** Keep distance, cast offense from range, `./ai/uo meditate` to
recover mana when safe. Reagent management matters — see
`../lore/items-and-resources.md` for what each reagent is and
`SKILL.md`'s spell quick-reference for common spell reagent costs.

**Start:** Buy a spellbook and basic reagents from a mage-shop NPC, train
Magery and EvalInt together by casting low-circle spells repeatedly
(Magic Arrow / Heal are common early gain-farmers), meditate to recover
between casts.

## Tamer

**Core skills:** Animal Taming (35), Animal Lore (2), Veterinary (39), often
paired with Magery (25) for a "tamer-mage" hybrid.

**Why these pair:** Animal Lore tells you a wild creature's real stats before
you commit to taming it (a failed tame attempt on a creature above your skill
can be dangerous). Veterinary keeps your tamed pets alive via bandages.

**Playstyle:** Tame a creature suited to your skill level (weaker/younger
creatures are safer early), then fight alongside it rather than in melee
yourself. Never attempt to tame something well above your current skill.

**Start:** Find a low-difficulty tameable animal (rabbits, cats — check
`../lore/bestiary.md` for taming-difficulty notes), use Animal Taming on it
repeatedly from a safe approach.

## Crafter

**Core skills:** pick one production skill — Blacksmithing (7), Tailoring
(34), Carpentry (11), or Tinkering (37) — paired with a matching gathering
skill: Mining (45) feeds Blacksmithing, Lumberjacking (44) feeds Carpentry/
Fletching, and Tailoring largely runs on purchased or hunted cloth/leather.

**Why these pair:** Self-sufficient material supply means training and
production don't depend on buying raw materials from other players/NPCs.

**Playstyle:** Non-combat. Gather, craft, sell. Low risk, steady skill/gold
progress, valuable to other players (including human ones — see `SKILL.md`
Roleplay section on being social).

**Start:** Buy basic tools (pick/shovel for Mining, hatchet for Lumberjacking,
the relevant crafting tool for your production skill) from a tool vendor,
gather raw material, craft repeatedly — crafting attempts that fail still
consume some material but still roll for skill gain.

## Bard

**Core skills:** Musicianship (29) underlies everything, paired with
Provocation (22), Peacemaking (9), and/or Discordance (15) depending on
whether you want to turn enemies on each other, calm them, or debuff them.

**Why these pair:** All three combat-bard skills require an instrument and a
successful Musicianship check before they can succeed — Musicianship is the
mandatory foundation, the others are the payoff.

**Playstyle:** Support/crowd-control rather than direct damage. Often paired
with a warrior or tamer build rather than played solo at low skill.

**Start:** Buy an instrument (lute, drum, harp, etc.) from a bard-supply NPC,
train Musicianship first by playing near safe targets before layering in
Provocation/Peacemaking/Discordance.

## Thief / Rogue

**Core skills:** Stealing (33), Hiding (21), Snooping (28), Lockpicking (24),
Stealth (47).

**Why these pair:** Hiding and Stealth let you approach and leave unseen;
Snooping and Stealing are the payoff; Lockpicking opens containers once
you've got to them.

**Playstyle:** High risk. Failed Stealing attempts on a mobile can flag you
Criminal (attackable without penalty) or trigger guards in town. Practice on
your own locked containers and low-value targets before attempting anything
that matters. Reread `SKILL.md` Safety Rule 1 — this applies doubly to a
thief build.

**Start:** Train Hiding and Snooping on safe/neutral targets first; only
attempt live Stealing once Hiding is reliable enough to escape after.

## Necromancer

**Core skills:** Necromancy (49), Spirit Speak (32), Focus (50), often paired
with a melee skill for a "death knight" hybrid or Magery for spell variety.

**Why these pair:** Spirit Speak fuels necromancer spell effectiveness;
Focus supports summoned undead and personal stats.

**Playstyle:** Darker utility kit — curses, undead summons, life drain.
Often disliked by town guards/Innocent NPCs if played visibly evil; roleplay
accordingly per `SKILL.md`.

**Start:** Learn necromancer spells from a necromancer trainer/spellbook,
cast low-tier spells repeatedly near safe targets to build Necromancy and
Spirit Speak together.

## Paladin

**Core skills:** Chivalry (51) plus a weapon skill (Swordsmanship/Macing/
Fencing) and Tactics (27), often Healing (17) too.

**Why these pair:** Chivalry is a prayer-based buff/heal/damage kit that
supplements melee rather than replacing it — this is a warrior chassis with
holy utility layered on.

**Playstyle:** Melee-first, like Warrior, with Chivalry abilities as
situational buffs/heals. See `combat.md` for the shared melee loop.

**Start:** Train the weapon skill and Tactics as a warrior would, layer in
Chivalry once basic melee is functional.

## Samurai

**Core skills:** Bushido (52) plus a weapon skill, typically Swordsmanship
or Fencing (katana/no-dachi/tessen are traditional Bushido weapons).

**Why these pair:** Bushido grants combat forms and defensive stances that
scale with the weapon skill, similar in structure to Paladin's Chivalry
layer but combat-focused rather than holy.

**Playstyle:** Melee-first, honor-focused roleplay. See `combat.md`.

**Start:** Train weapon skill and Tactics first, layer in Bushido forms once
combat basics are solid.

## Ninja

**Core skills:** Ninjitsu (53) plus Hiding (21) and Stealth (47), often a
light weapon skill (Fencing/Wrestling) for combat.

**Why these pair:** Ninjitsu's toolkit (smoke bombs, animal form, backstab-
style openers) depends on approaching unseen — Hiding/Stealth are the
delivery mechanism.

**Playstyle:** Ambush and mobility over sustained toe-to-toe combat.

**Start:** Train Hiding/Stealth as a Thief would, layer Ninjitsu abilities
in once you can reliably approach a target unseen.
